# Clinical trajectory stress test

## Purpose and evidential status

This notebook documents the application of the active-inference transition framework to the clinical symptom histories released with Jin, Zeidman, Friston, and Kotov (2023), *Inferring trajectories of patients with psychosis: A dynamic causal modeling study*. The aim is not to validate the active-inference mechanism. The released observations contain symptoms only: they do not record engagement and withdrawal policies, sampled outcomes, preferences, or policy precision. The application therefore tests a reduced nonlinear transition surface derived from the model against simpler predictive accounts.

The analysis is implemented in `Python_code/clinical_trajectory_comparison.py`. It uses only NumPy, SciPy, and Matplotlib and writes aggregate outputs to `outputs/clinical_trajectories/`.

## Released data and provenance

`VALIDATION/data/jin_2023/Data_for_Model.mat` contains nine selected patients, each represented by 209 reconstructed weeks across psychosis, depression, and mania. The MATLAB metadata identifies the upstream file as `all628_week_restacked.mat`, generated by the script `mass_data_selection` on 10 June 2021. The full 628-person source file is not part of the released supplement.

Three patients have missing values. Across all finite adjacent same-symptom pairs, only 34 of 5,401 observations change from the previous week. The forecasting table contains 5,414 finite targets because an intercept or fallback prediction can still be issued when the immediately preceding value of that symptom is missing. This imbalance makes percentage accuracy uninformative and makes held-out probabilistic scoring essential.

## Models

All predictive results use leave-one-patient-out validation. Each model is fitted to eight patients and makes one-step predictions for the ninth, conditional on information available through the current week.

1. **Intercept model.** Pooled symptom prevalences with a Jeffreys Beta prior.
2. **First-order Markov model.** Separate smoothed probabilities of each symptom next week conditional on whether it is present this week.
3. **Linear burden model.** A pooled logistic transition model using the current three-symptom burden, mapped to the order-parameter scale from −1 to 1.
4. **Two-state Bernoulli HMM.** A pooled hidden-state comparator fitted by multi-start expectation-maximisation with symptom-specific emissions.
5. **Active-inference-derived nonlinear map.** Current symptom burden is inserted into the mean-field response

   `m(t+1) = tanh[J z(t) + h]`,

   followed by symptom-specific Bernoulli emissions. `J` is the effective positive feedback and `h` is the effective field. These are observational transition parameters, not recovered POMDP policy precision and preference-field parameters.

The nonlinear self-consistency equation can support multiple fixed points only when `J > 1` and the field lies inside the corresponding fold wedge. The code reports this diagnostic in every cross-validation fold rather than equating `J > 1` with empirical bistability.

## Held-out results

The overall leave-one-patient-out ranking is:

| Model | Log loss | Brier score | Log loss on the 34 changes |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| First-order Markov | 0.040 | 0.007 | 4.607 |
| Active-inference-derived nonlinear | 0.390 | 0.123 | 4.630 |
| Linear burden | 0.432 | 0.129 | 3.104 |
| Two-state HMM | 0.459 | 0.147 | 1.627 |
| Intercept | 0.600 | 0.199 | 0.756 |

The Markov model wins decisively overall because almost every week is unchanged. That result is not evidence that transitions are unimportant; it shows that the released subset is dominated by dwell time. Conversely, the Markov and nonlinear models assign especially poor probabilities to the 34 changes. The intercept and HMM are less accurate overall but less surprised when changes occur. A model can therefore look excellent on aggregate while failing precisely where a transition theory should be most informative.

Across the nine training folds, the nonlinear coupling estimate exceeds one, but only two fitted `(J, h)` pairs lie inside the fold wedge. The apparent positive feedback is therefore not stable evidence that the observed histories occupy a bistable regime.

## Audit of the supplied Lorenz fits

`data/jin_2023/GCM_nosology.mat` contains the original in-sample DCM fits. We observe that the fitted baseline Rayleigh parameters range from 15.31 to 22.63, below the classical stability threshold of approximately 24.74. The time-varying effective parameter crosses that threshold only for patients 5 and 7, for 14 and 41 weeks respectively. Every supplied latent trajectory has one sign/lobe crossing, associated with initial settling rather than recurrent itinerancy.

This analysis does not refute the Lorenz DCM. It simply shows that the fitted system generally operates in fixed-point-like regimes and that the released fits demonstrate flexible in-sample reconstruction, not held-out superiority over persistence or another transition mechanism. The Lorenz fits are therefore displayed descriptively and excluded from the predictive ranking (and comparing their variational free energies or in-sample residuals with held-out Bernoulli log scores would be invalid).

## Interpretation

The released clinical sequences provide a useful boundary test:

- they are compatible with long-lived symptom regimes;
- they do not identify action-dependent learning or separate preference from precision;
- they contain too few changes to distinguish a mechanistic cusp confidently from ordinary persistence;
- the active-inference-derived map adds nonlinear structure but does not improve on the Markov baseline;
- the rare transition weeks, rather than overall accuracy, are the decisive empirical target for future data.

A stronger test requires the full cohort or a new intensive dataset with many prospectively observed transitions and direct measurements of actions, outcomes, perceived value, and choice stochasticity. Model comparison should then use a shared observation model, rolling forecasts, parameter recovery, and cross-generative recovery across Markov/HMM, Lorenz, and active-inference-derived alternatives.
